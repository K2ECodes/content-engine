## 2. Shopify's quarter shows checkout moving inside AI agents, and investors punished it anyway

- Date: reported Nov 3, 2026.
- Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001594805/000159480526000067/exhibit991pressreleaseq320.htm and https://ppc.land/shopify-revenue-gains-32-to-3-7bn-as-ai-referred-orders-rise-3-3x/
- Summary: Shopify reported Q3 2026 revenue of $3.74B, up 32% from $2.84B a year earlier, and gross merchandise volume of $119.1B, up from $92.0B. ## 3. UPS tells investors AI and robotics are covering peak season, not more seasonal hires

- Date: Q3 2026 earnings call Oct 27, 2026; seasonal hiring and volume figures reported through early Nov 2026.
- Source: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/ups-to-hire-more-than-100k-seasonal-workers-for-peak and https://www.wabe.org/ups-expects-to-lean-on-ai-and-seasonal-workers-during-the-holidays/
- Summary: On its Q3 call, UPS said 70% of its packages now move through distribution and fulfillment buildings that mix autonomous robotics with AI, up from a year earlier, and CEO Carol Tome said daily volume is expected to climb 25% from Q3 to Q4. UPS plans to hire roughly 100,000 seasonal workers this year, down from about 110,000 last year and 125,000 in 2024, a drop the company ties directly to automation, alongside a separate plan to cut 30,000 positions in 2026 to reduce costs. Quarterly volume is still expected to be down year over year because UPS deliberately reduced Amazon volume earlier this year.
- Why operators should care: the country's largest parcel carrier just told its own investors that AI and robotics, not more temp labor, are how it plans to absorb the holiday surge, which is the same math a smaller operator should be running before assuming extra hands are the only way through peak.

## 4. A second Los Angeles and Long Beach port deadline moves again, now to Nov 20

- Date: extension reported Nov 7, 2026; original deadline was Oct 31, 2026.
- Source: https://gvwire.com/2026/11/07/employers-watchmen-at-california-ports-extend-contract-talks/ and https://www.freightwaves.com/news/port-strike-delayed-again-at-los-angeles-long-beach-ports
- Summary: The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26, which represents roughly 250 watchmen who control terminal gates and guard cargo at the Los Angeles and Long Beach ports, have pushed their contract deadline a 4th time, from Oct 31 to Nov 4, then Nov 6, and now to Nov 20, 2026. The affected terminals handle about 85% of the cargo at Long Beach and about 33% at Los Angeles. This is a separate contract from the main West Coast longshore deal, which runs through 2028, but the PMA has warned other ILWU members are expected to honor a Local 26 picket line, and the union has already authorized a strike.
- Why operators should care: a walkout by roughly 2% of the ILWU workforce could still disrupt more than half the cargo moving through the busiest port complex in the country, right as peak season freight is already in motion, and the deadline keeps sliding closer to the holidays instead of away from them.

## 5. A freight industry voice ties the broker crackdown directly to the $604M C.H. Robinson verdict

- Date: remarks given Nov 2, 2026 at Optym's Convergence 2026 conference; reported Nov 7, 2026.
- Source: https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fuller-big-carriers-fmcsa-crackdown-permanent
- Summary: FreightWaves CEO Craig Fuller told the Convergence 2026 conference that large carriers want federal enforcement against non-domiciled commercial driver's licenses and fraudulent carriers to stay permanent, not ease up under the next administration. He tied that push to a Supreme Court ruling that opened freight brokers to state law negligent hiring claims, and to the $604M jury verdict against C.H. Robinson over a fatal 2021 crash, where the jury split fault 45% to the driver, 32% to the carrier, and 23% to the broker for how it vetted that carrier.
- Why operators should care: a broker's carrier vetting decision just cost over $600M in a single case, which is exactly the kind of outbound action that needs a documented, human approval step, not a gut check, no matter how good a carrier's safety rating looks on paper.
