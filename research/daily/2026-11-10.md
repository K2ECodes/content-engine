## 2. A 2-year tariff exclusion on 178 Chinese product lines lapsed last night, no extension announced

- Date: lapsed 11:59pm ET Nov 9, 2026.
- Source: https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2025/november/ustr-extends-exclusions-china-section-301-tariffs-related-forced-technology-transfer-investigation and https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/section-301-china-exclusions-expire-november-10-2026-eight-week-snap-back-playbook
- Summary: 178 Section 301 tariff exclusions on Chinese goods (164 product-specific, 14 for solar manufacturing equipment, filed under HTS 9903.88.69 and .70) expired at 11:59pm ET Nov 9, 2026 with no extension from USTR. Imports entering on or after Nov 10 lose the exclusion and revert to the standard Section 301 rate, typically 25%, with some categories also carrying a 7.5% List 4A rate. This is separate from the broader US-China tariff truce, which both sides already extended to Jan 10, 2027, so many importers assumed the tariff story was settled when this specific carve-out wasn't covered.
- Why operators should care: any brand or 3PL still landing covered goods thinks they're paying the truce rate when, as of today, they're not, and the gap shows up as margin loss the invoice doesn't flag on its own.

## 3. Amazon plans its biggest holiday hiring jump in 3 years while Walmart and Target stay quiet

- Source: https://geekwire.com/2026/amazon-plans-record-300k-holiday-hires-as-the-rest-of-retail-pulls-back/ and https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/amazon-to-hire-more-than-300000-in-holiday-season-up-20-from-last-year
- Summary: Amazon said it will hire over 300,000 seasonal, part-time and full-time US workers this holiday season, up 20% from the 250,000 it sought in 2025 and its first recruiting increase in 3 years. Walmart and Target have both held back on announcing seasonal headcount numbers, a departure from past years, with Walmart executives talking about keeping headcount flat going forward to capture AI efficiencies. - Why operators should care: the retailer spending the most on AI and robotics is also the 1 hiring the most people for peak, a reminder that "AI or labor" is the wrong framing when planning staffing for the season ahead.

## 4. Walmart's AI shopping-agent gateway still isn't fully live, 2 days before its first Black Friday sale window

- Date: gateway announced Oct 21, 2026; agent profiles still pending as of Nov 10, 2026.
- Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/while-amazon-blocks-ai-agents-walmart-rolls-out-the-welcome-mat/ and https://retaildive.com/news/walmart-welcomes-external-ai-shopping-agents-terms-boundaries/833723
- Summary: Walmart opened an early-beta Agent Gateway letting outside AI agents such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta's Muse shop Walmart.com and Sam's Club on a customer's behalf, with no access to payment-card data and spending limits such as $50, $100 or $200. - Why operators should care: the retailer most open to agent-driven shopping still hasn't shipped the spending guardrails it promised, which is exactly the gap an e-commerce brand should check in its own store before an AI agent, not a person, places the order.
