## 1. BCG's annual Black Friday survey finds 1 in 5 shoppers actively distrust advertised discounts, and AI review summaries are the least trusted trust signal on the list

- Date: Nov 4, 2026.
- Source: https://www.bcg.com/press/4november2026-consumer-trust-black-friday-discounts-promotion-fatigue and https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/black-friday-shoppers-are-growing-skeptical-of-holiday-discounts-new-survey-finds-110526.html
- Summary: BCG surveyed 10,220 consumers across 10 countries and found only 54% trust that advertised Black Friday prices are genuinely lower, 1 in 5 actively distrust them, and the discount shoppers now expect to call a deal "good" eased to around 30%. Retailer reputation and money-back guarantees scored highest for building trust, verified purchase reviews next, and AI-generated review summaries scored lowest of every factor tested. On AI shopping tools specifically, shoppers trust AI most for comparing prices across retailers and least for a bare recommendation with no way to verify it.
- Why operators should care: this is a direct rebuttal to "just add AI" as a trust fix, shoppers want AI that shows its work (price history, a comparison they can check), not a bot that vouches for a deal.
- Post angles: (li-personal, static, belief_shift, hot-take) Korede POV: the data says shoppers trust an AI that shows its math, not one that just tells them to trust it, and that's the whole difference between a chatbot and a system. ## 2. A holiday CX survey finds 82% of shoppers expect a refund or credit if a brand's AI breaks a promise, and 44% will abandon support after waiting 30 minutes

- Date: Nov 4, 2026.
- Source: https://retailwire.com/discussion/bad-ai-bots-anger-customers-brands-must-stand-behind-chatbot-promises/ (citing Forethought AI Agents by Zendesk, "State of AI in CX 2026: Holiday Report," 1,039 US adults, fielded Sep 4 to 8, 2026)
- Summary: 53% of shoppers expect AI to save them at least 1 hour this season and 24% expect at least 3 hours, but trust has a ceiling, only 18% would let AI make a purchase over $500 on its own. 82% expect a brand to make good if its AI gets something wrong, 44% will walk away from support after a 30 minute wait, and 15% say a wrong item from a bad AI recommendation would ruin their holiday shopping more than anything else.
- Why operators should care: the AI doesn't have to be perfect, but someone has to own the fallout when it's wrong, which is the entire argument for keeping a person on the hook for what an AI promises a customer.

## 3. Mastercard's Agent Pay for Machines reframes the Black Friday weekend fraud question as delegation, not just theft

- Date: Nov 18, 2026.
- Source: https://thepaypers.com/payments/expert-views/black-friday-2026-when-the-shopper-is-an-ai-agent
- Summary: Paula Albu's piece walks through what changes for merchants and fraud teams once the shopper is an AI agent, built around Mastercard's Agent Pay for Machines (launched in June 2026 to permission, orchestrate and settle machine speed payments). Its core argument: most platforms' terms make the customer liable for an agent's mistake, but in practice the dispute lands on the merchant, and more than 55% of January 2026 refund claims in Riskified's data came from holiday orders, a preview of how fast agent related refund fraud could scale.
- Why operators should care: whoever's agent makes the mistake, the merchant tends to eat the dispute, which is a direct argument for never letting an agent complete a sale or a refund without a person signing off first.
- Post angles: (li-personal, static, belief_shift, hot-take) Korede POV: everyone's excited about agents buying things, almost nobody's talking about who eats the dispute when the agent gets it wrong, and that's the gap every system we build closes.
