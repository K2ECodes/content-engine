What if a zombie outbreak started while you were closing a supermarket all alone? It's 10.52 at night. Eight minutes to closing. Then your phone starts screaming. Emergency alert. A deadly infection is spreading through the city. Stay inside. Do not go out. Through the glass, you see why. People are sprinting across the car park and the ones chasing them never get tired. Your first instinct? Grab your keys and drive home. Don't. Every road out of town is already jammed. Out there, you'd be trapped in a car. Because without realizing it, you're standing in one of the best places to survive this. 30,000 products. A steel shutter. A loading dock you can lock. But only if you fix one problem in the next five minutes. The whole front of the shop is glass. Every light is still on. And they've noticed. You slam the shutter button. It rolls down and jams. Knee height. Shutters like this have a hand chain. For when the motor fails. 40 pulls. 38, 39. Something grabs the bottom edge. Then let's go. 40. The shutter hits the floor. Shop rule one. If they can see you, they come. Lights off. In the dark, you count what you've got. Food for years. Water for months. For the first time tonight, you breathe. Then the night guard's walkie -talkie crackles on the counter. He went home at 10. Someone whispers into it. Who turned off the lights?
