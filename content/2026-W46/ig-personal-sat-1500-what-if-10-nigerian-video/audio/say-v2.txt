What if everyone on Earth woke up ten percent Nigerian? Not on paper. In the blood. Here's what would actually happen.
Day one. Every DNA test on the planet comes back the same: Nigeria. Eight point two billion people with Nigerian roots. Yesterday it was two hundred and thirty seven million.
Day two. The people who hated us are us now. Hate forums go quiet overnight. Family group chats get louder, and somebody's uncle forwards the same voice note to forty people.
Day three. Every parent on Earth asks the same question at the same time: why are you not a doctor yet? In Oslo, a nine year old gets the speech over a B plus.
Week one. Everyone wants the green passport. The embassy queues wrap around the block. At the window, the answer is short: you need a Nigerian parent, and ten percent doesn't count.
Week two. Jollof wars go global. London hosts a cook-off with four thousand people. Nigeria wins. Ghana says theirs is still better.
Month one. Afrobeats is just called music now. People already found Nigerian artists over one billion times on Spotify last year. This year, your grandmother knows the dance.
Year one. Money flows to Lagos. The diaspora already sends home twenty one billion dollars a year. Now everybody has an aunty in Lagos asking them to send something small.
Ten years later. Racism doesn't survive the family reunion. It's hard to hate people you share jollof with every Sunday.
