What if every container ship on Earth stopped for thirty days? Not slowed down. Stopped. Around eighty percent of world trade by volume moves by sea, according to the United Nations.
Day one. Ships drop anchor outside every major port, from Rotterdam to Shanghai to Los Angeles. Crews wait for orders that never come. Insurers start making very nervous phone calls.
We've seen a tiny version of this before. In 2021, one ship, the Ever Given, got stuck in the Suez Canal for six days. It held up about nine point six billion dollars of goods, every single day.
Week one. Factories run out of parts. A car plant stops the whole line because one small chip is missing. Phone makers send workers home.
Week two. Ports go quiet. Thousands of trucks sit in the yard with nothing to pick up. Drivers start looking for other work.
Month one. Shelves thin out, prices climb, and every brand finds out how many weeks of stock it really has. Most find out it's fewer than they thought.
