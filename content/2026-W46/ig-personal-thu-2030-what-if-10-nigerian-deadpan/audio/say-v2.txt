What if everyone on Earth woke up ten percent Nigerian? Not a little bit. Exactly ten percent. And your family found out first.
Day one. An aunty you've never met calls you at six A M. She knows your full name, your salary, and that you're still single. She hangs up before you can ask who she is.
Day three. Your mum calls you "this boy" in the middle of the supermarket. Nine times. Now strangers are calling you "this boy" too.
Week one. The office party has two pots of jollof. By three P M it's a full argument about whose is better. It ends with H R, a written warning, and nobody returning the plates.
Week three. Every house on your street buys a generator, just in case. Nobody's power has gone off. They argue about whose is louder anyway.
Month two. Weddings now last three days. You have four this weekend. You've spent two thousand four hundred euros on matching outfits, and you've only met one of the couples.
Month six. The visa queue at the embassy stretches forty-one kilometres. People bring folding chairs and coolers of food. At the front, the answer is still no.
Year one. "We have food at home" becomes the most-said sentence on Earth. Fast food sales drop thirty-one percent. Burger chains quietly add jollof to the menu.
Year five. Every stadium plays Afrobeats at half time. Even funerals have a D J now. Nobody remembers what a quiet afternoon sounds like.
Year ten. You finally become a doctor. Your mum hugs you, cries, and asks when you're getting married. What should happen next?
