What if the internet went down tomorrow, everywhere, for thirty days? No Wi-Fi, no mobile data, no cloud. Just silence.
Hour one. Your card stops working at the coffee shop. The barista shrugs. Only fourteen percent of payments in the US are still made in cash, according to the Federal Reserve, and you're not one of them.
Day one. More than ninety-nine percent of the data between continents travels through cables on the sea floor. They're still there. Nothing is moving through them.
So banks, airlines, and hospitals go back to paper. Pilots get printed flight plans. Doctors write prescriptions by hand again, and nobody can read them.
Day three. Stores reorder by phone and fax. Shelves start to thin out, starting with bread, batteries and toilet paper.
Week two. Neighbours trade favours. Somebody's grandad becomes the most important person on the street, because he still has a radio and knows how to use it.
Day thirty. The internet comes back on. Billions of people log in at once, and nobody remembers their passwords.
