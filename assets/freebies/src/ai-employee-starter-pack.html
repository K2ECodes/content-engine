Set up a skill in 10 minutes

Each skill is a short instruction you save once and reuse every day. You don't need code, just a paid Claude or ChatGPT plan.

STEP 1

Create a Project in Claude or ChatGPT and name it after the job, for example "Inbox triage".

STEP 2

Paste the instruction into the project's instructions. Or drop the 5 skill folders from ai-starter-skills.zip into Claude.

STEP 3

Run it on real work for 1 week. Fix the instruction every time it gets something wrong.

01

/inbox-triage · Inbox triage, drafts only

What it does

Sorts the emails you paste in, tells you who wants what by when, and drafts the replies that need you. It never sends.

When to use it

First thing in the morning, or after a day away.

You approve

Every reply before it goes out, and every flagged email.

Paste into Claude or ChatGPTInbox triage
You're my inbox assistant. When I paste emails: 1. Sort each 1 into: Act today, This week, FYI, Ignore. 2. Give 1 line per email: who, what they want, by when. 3. For Act today and This week, draft a reply in my voice: short, plain, polite, no filler. 4. List anything about money, contracts, complaints or deadlines at the top, under FLAGGED. Rules: never send anything. Never promise a date, price or refund. If you're unsure what I'd say, write [ASK ME].
02

/meeting-prep · Meeting prep brief

What it does

Turns the invite, past emails and your notes into a 1-page brief you can read in 3 minutes.

When to use it

15 minutes before a sales call, client review or interview.

You approve

Nothing goes out. You read it and you decide.

Paste into Claude or ChatGPTMeeting prep
Prepare a 1-page brief for my meeting. I'll paste the invite, past emails, my notes and their website text. Give me: - Who's in the room and what each person cares about - Where things stand: last thing agreed, open items - 3 questions I should ask - 1 objection to be ready for - The outcome I should push for Only use what I give you. If something is missing, list it under GAPS instead of guessing.
03

/follow-up-writer · Follow-up writer

What it does

Turns rough call notes or a transcript into a clear follow-up: what was agreed, who owns what, and the next date.

When to use it

Right after any call where something was agreed.

You approve

The email before it's sent, and every [CONFIRM] item.

Paste into Claude or ChatGPTFollow-up
Write a follow-up email from my call notes below. Structure: - 1 line of thanks, no fluff - What we agreed, as bullets - Who does what, by when - The next step and its date Keep it under 150 words and in my voice. Don't add anything that isn't in the notes. Mark any item where the owner or date is unclear with [CONFIRM]. Draft only. Never send.

04

/weekly-numbers · Weekly numbers summary

What it does

Reads the numbers you export each week (sales, orders, cash, leads) and tells you what moved and what to ask about.

When to use it

Monday morning, before the team meeting.

You approve

Any decision it suggests. Spot-check 1 number against the source every week.

Paste into Claude or ChatGPTWeekly numbers
I'll paste this week's numbers and last week's. Write a summary for a busy owner: 1. The 3 numbers that moved most, with the change 2. 1 likely reason for each, clearly marked as a guess 3. Anything in the data itself that looks wrong 4. 2 questions I should ask my team Show the math for every change. Never invent a number. If a figure is missing, say so.
05

/sop-writer · SOP writer

What it does

Turns a voice note, a screen-recording transcript or messy notes into steps a new hire can follow.

When to use it

Every time you catch yourself explaining the same task twice.

You approve

The final SOP. Have the person who does the task test it once before the team uses it.

Paste into Claude or ChatGPTSOP writer
Turn my notes below into a standard operating procedure. Format: - Title, purpose in 1 line, who does it, how often - Numbered steps, 1 action per step, plain words - What "done" looks like - Common mistakes and how to avoid them Before you finish, ask me up to 3 questions about gaps. Don't invent steps I didn't describe.

Run them like a new hire, not a tool

A

Drafts first, always

Every skill starts drafts-only. It earns more freedom after 2 clean weeks, on low-risk work only.

B

1 owner per skill

Name who checks its work each week. A skill with no owner drifts until a customer notices.

C

Feed it real examples

Add 3 of your best past replies, briefs or SOPs to the project. It learns your standard fast.

D

Fix the instruction, not the output

Don't just correct the draft. Add 1 line to the instruction so the mistake doesn't repeat.

The permission sheet

Fill 1 in for each AI employee. Tick every line that applies, cross out what doesn't fit, add your own, and pin it where the team can see it.

AI employee
Owner
Reviewed on

Does alone

Inside your walls, nothing leaves.

  • Read and sort what it's given
  • Summarize emails, calls and documents
  • Draft replies, posts and SOPs
  • Prepare briefs, lists and reports
  • Flag risks, gaps and deadlines
  • Suggest next steps
  •  

Needs your yes

It prepares, a person approves.

  • Sending anything to a customer, client or supplier
  • Paying, refunding or changing a price
  • Deleting or archiving records
  • Posting anything in public
  • Quotes, contracts and anything signed
  • Booking or cancelling for other people
  •  

Never does

No exceptions, even if asked.

  • Sees your passwords or card details
  • Moves money on its own
  • Promises a price, date or refund
  • Makes legal, medical or HR calls
  • Presents a guess as a fact
  • Uses data it wasn't given
  •  

When in doubt, it asks. A draft costs nothing to throw away. A wrong send can cost a client.

How a line moves up

WEEKS 1 TO 2

Everything is a draft. You read all of it and fix the instruction as you go.

WEEKS 3 TO 4

Low-risk internal work runs alone. You spot-check a few each week.

AFTER THAT

Review this sheet monthly. Move a line only when it's been right every time.
