Support Handoff Map

The 5 moments

For each: what the bot listens for, what it says, and what the person picking it up gets. The customer should never have to repeat themselves.

01

An angry customer

Listen for

A 2nd complaint on the same order, all caps, "this is the 3rd time", a threat to leave a review or dispute the charge.

The bot says

"I'm sorry this has gone wrong. I'm passing you to a person now, with everything you've told me."

The person gets

The order, the full chat, what's been tried, and a note that the customer is upset.

02

Someone who wants to cancel

Listen for

Cancel, stop my subscription, close my account, return everything.

The bot says

"Before anything changes, someone from our team will take this. They can already see your order."

The person gets

Order history, the reason given, and what they're allowed to offer to keep the customer.

03

A refund over your limit

Listen for

A refund above the amount you set, a 2nd refund request, a high-value order, a damaged item.

The bot says

"This needs a quick check from our team. You'll hear back by [time]."

The person gets

The amount, the order, the policy line that applies, and past refunds for this customer.

04

A question the bot isn't sure about

Listen for

No clear answer in your policies, anything about safety or allergies, a promise your policy doesn't cover.

The bot says

"I don't want to guess on this. A person will answer you by [time]."

The person gets

The question, what the bot found, and exactly where it got stuck.

05

Anyone who asks for a human

Listen for

Human, real person, agent, call me. Asked once is enough.

The bot says

"Of course. Connecting you now." No arguing, no "let me try 1 more thing".

The person gets

The full chat and the order, so they start where the bot stopped.

A good handoff, every time

  • The customer never repeats their order number or their problem.
  • The bot says when they'll hear back, and the time is true.
  • Out of hours, it says so plainly, and gives the time someone will reply.
  • Peak weekends have a named person on handoffs, not "whoever's around".
  • Once a week, someone reads 10 handoffs and fixes what the bot could have answered.
